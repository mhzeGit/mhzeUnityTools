# Multi-View Layout

Default: exactly three drawings in one horizontal row, showing one fixed object in its natural resting orientation.

1. **Three-quarter hero:** recognizable silhouette and useful depth, with an angle appropriate to the item.
2. **True orthographic side:** camera level, perpendicular to the defined side, zero perspective. Show the actual profile and state; do not flatten or lower parts to simplify this view.
3. **True orthographic front or top:** choose the view that explains the form. Front and side are normally 90 degrees apart, but front is not universally edge-on. Top can suit flat or spread-out items.

Define front/side when ambiguity matters: an axe can use broadside and edge-on, a chair side and front, and a plate thickness and top. Do not copy axe-specific directions onto every prop.

## Useful prompt wording

- ONE identical object from three cameras; preserve geometry, proportions, pose, state, parts and marks.
- Side camera perfectly level, zero perspective; a side view must never be another three-quarter view.
- Top camera directly vertically overhead, zero perspective; do not show a tilted three-quarter projection.
- Orthographic views use the same pixels per unit; a narrow profile stays narrow rather than being enlarged to fill a panel.

In a level elevation, a flat horizontal surface should not expose its top face. Curved surfaces, bevels and raised parts remain visible according to their geometry. Inspect the pixels: prompt wording does not guarantee camera accuracy.

For N items, use N rows and three columns, one design per row. Match scale within each row, not across unrelated items. Prefer dedicated sheets when a batch obscures complex parts. Show complete silhouettes, generous margins and tiny THREE-QUARTER, SIDE and FRONT or TOP labels. Use a quiet neutral backdrop unless requested otherwise.
